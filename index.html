4 Major Types of Yoga

Karma Yoga

Karma Yoga is the path of selfless action and service, one of the four classical paths of yoga, aimed at spiritual liberation. It emphasizes performing duties without attachment to the results, offering actions as a form of worship, and dedicating oneself to work without ego or desire for personal gain.

Bhakti Yoga

Bhakti Yoga, also known as the "path of love and devotion," is a spiritual practice within Hinduism that emphasizes loving devotion to a personal deity as a means to achieve liberation (moksha). It involves cultivating a deep, personal relationship with the divine, often through practices like prayer, chanting, and selfless service.

Raja Yoga

Rāja Yoga, also known as Ashtanga Yoga or the "Royal Path," is a system of yoga emphasizing mental discipline and meditation for self-realization. It is considered one of the four classical paths of yoga, focusing on controlling the mind and its fluctuations to achieve inner peace and spiritual liberation.

Jñāna Yoga

Jnana Yoga, also known as the "path of knowledge," is one of the three classical paths to liberation (moksha) in Hinduism, alongside Karma Yoga and Bhakti Yoga. It emphasizes the use of intellect and self-inquiry to realize the true nature of reality and one's own self. Through discrimination between the real and the unreal, and by understanding the interconnectedness of all things, practitioners aim to transcend the limitations of the ego and achieve liberation
